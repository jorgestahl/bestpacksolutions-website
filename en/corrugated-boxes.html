How do you choose the right flute and ECT?

The flute is the inner wave of the corrugated board and defines thickness and cushioning; the ECT (Edge Crush Test) measures how much vertical compression the box withstands when stacked. Choosing correctly avoids crushed boxes or wasted material.

Flutes and strength (ECT): the heart of a good box

Corrugated board is made of an inner wave called the flute glued between two flat liners. C flute is the most common in shipping boxes; B flute offers a better printing surface and stacking; BC double wall provides maximum strength for heavy loads; and E or F flutes are used for fine packaging and die-cut displays. The ECT (Edge Crush Test) indicates how much vertical compression the box can bear: the taller the stack and the heavier the load, the higher the ECT you need.

What are ECT and flute in corrugated board?

The ECT (Edge Crush Test) measures the box's vertical compression strength. The flute is the inner wave of the corrugated board: C and B flutes provide good stacking, BC double wall delivers maximum strength, and E or F flutes are used for fine packaging and printing.
