New vs. Recycled Pallets: Which One Should You Buy?

There is no single answer: the best pallet depends on your load, your budget and your logistics. We compare both options so you can decide with data.

New pallets: maximum strength and service life

New pallets are built from fresh lumber and components with no prior use. They offer the highest structural strength, dimensional consistency and service life, which makes them ideal for:

  • Heavy or high-value loads.
  • Automated lines that require exact dimensions.
  • Export shipments (with heat treatment, HT).
  • Industries with high hygiene standards, such as food and pharmaceutical.

Their main drawback is cost: they are more expensive than recycled pallets and have a larger raw-material footprint.

Recycled pallets: cost and sustainability

Recycled pallets are used pallets that are recovered, inspected and reconditioned to return to service. Their advantages:

  • Lower cost, down to a fraction of the price of a new pallet.
  • Sustainability: they reduce waste and make use of existing lumber.
  • Fast availability for standard loads and internal movements.

The trade-off: strength and service life depend on the condition of the recovered pallet. That is why it is essential to buy from a supplier that inspects and grades them by quality.

Quick comparison

New → greater durability, precision and strength. Best for export, automation and critical loads.

Recycled → lower cost and more sustainable. Best for internal flows, standard loads and budget optimization.

The middle option: hybrid pallets

There is a middle ground. Hybrid pallets combine new and reconditioned components, balancing strength and cost. They are an excellent alternative when you need more durability than a recycled pallet but want to keep spending in check.

How to decide?

Ask yourself: will the pallet leave your plant (export, delivery to a customer) or stay in an internal loop? How much weight must it carry? Does your line require exact dimensions?
